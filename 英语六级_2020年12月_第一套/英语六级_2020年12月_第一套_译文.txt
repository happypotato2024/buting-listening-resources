[p30] 原文：How can we live more basically? Firstly, we must ruthlessly cut the unnecessary objects out of our lives. [p34] 原文：Now, believe it or not, people sometimes lie in order to maintain a good, honest reputation — even if it hurts them to do so. At least, this is what a team of scientists is suggesting, with evidence to prove it.
[p34] 译文：现在，不管你信不信，人们有时会撒谎，以保持良好、诚实的声誉——即使这样做会伤害他们。至少，这是一组科学家的建议，并有证据证明这一点。

[p35] 原文：Picture this scenario: You often drive for work and can be compensated for up to 400 miles per month. Most people at your company drive about 300 miles each month. But this month, you drove 400 miles. How many miles do you think you'd claim in your expense report?
[p35] 译文：想象一下这样的场景：您经常开车上班，每月可以获得最多 400 英里的补偿。贵公司的大多数人每月行驶约 300 英里。但这个月，您开了 400 英里。您认为您会在费用报告中索取多少里程？

[p36] 原文：The scientists asked this exact question as part of the study we're discussing today. With surprising results, they found that 12% of respondents reported the distance they drove as less than the actual figure, giving an average answer of 384 miles. In other words, they lied about their number of miles, even though they would forfeit money they were owed.
[p36] 译文：作为我们今天讨论的研究的一部分，科学家们提出了这个确切的问题。结果令人惊讶，他们发现 12% 的受访者报告的驾驶距离少于实际数字，平均答案为 384 英里。换句话说，他们谎报了自己的里程数，尽管他们会没收欠他们的钱。

[p37] 原文：The researchers believe this was to seem honest, with the assumption being that others would be suspicious of a high expense claim. But why would people fabricate numbers to their own detriment? The researchers explain that many people care a great deal about their reputation and how they'll be judged by others. If they care enough, their concern about appearing honest and not losing the respect of others may be greater than their desire to actually be honest.
[p37] 译文：研究人员认为，这看起来很诚实，因为假设其他人会对高额费用索赔表示怀疑。但为什么人们会捏造数字来损害自己的利益呢？研究人员解释说，许多人非常关心自己的声誉以及他人如何评价他们。如果他们足够关心，他们对表现诚实和不失去他人尊重的担忧可能会大于他们真正诚实的愿望。

[p38] 原文：The researchers assert that the findings suggest that when people obtain very favorable outcomes, they anticipate other people's suspicious reactions and prefer lying and appearing honest to telling the truth and appearing as selfish liars.
[p38] 译文：研究人员声称，研究结果表明，当人们获得非常有利的结果时，他们会预见到其他人的可疑反应，并且更喜欢撒谎并表现出诚实，而不是说真话并表现得像自私的骗子。

[p39] 原文：So why is this research important? Well, experts generally agree there are two main types of lie — selfish lies and lies that are meant to benefit others. The first, as you may predict, is for selfish gain, such as submitting a fraudulent claim to an insurance company, while the second involves lying to help others or not offend others, for example, telling a friend whose outfit you don't like that they look great.
[p39] 译文：那么为什么这项研究很重要呢？嗯，专家们普遍认为谎言有两种主要类型——自私的谎言和旨在造福他人的谎言。正如您可能预料到的那样，第一个是为了自私，例如向保险公司提出欺诈性索赔，而第二个则涉及撒谎以帮助他人或不冒犯他人，例如告诉您不喜欢其服装的朋友，他们看起来很棒。

[p40] 原文：But the researchers are suggesting a third type of lying — lying to maintain a good reputation. Now this hypothesis is new and some skeptics argue that this isn't a whole new category of lie.
[p40] 译文：但研究人员提出了第三种谎言——为了维持良好声誉而撒谎。现在这个假设是新的，一些怀疑论者认为这并不是一个全新的谎言类别。

[p41] 原文：But the findings seem intuitive to me. After all, one of the main motivations for lying is to increase our worth in the eyes of others. So it seems highly likely that people will lie to seem honest.
[p41] 译文：但这些发现对我来说似乎很直观。毕竟，说谎的主要动机之一是提高我们在别人眼中的价值。因此，人们很可能会撒谎以显得诚实。

[p42] 译文：为什么老年人不喜欢新音乐？随着年龄的增长，我经常听到同龄人说“他们只是不再像以前那样创作出好音乐了”。为什么会发生这种情况？幸运的是，心理学可以为我们提供一些关于这个难题的见解。音乐品味早在 13 岁或 14 岁就开始结晶。到了 20 岁出头时，这些品味就已经相当牢固地固定下来了。

[p43] 原文：In fact, studies have found that by the time we turn 33, most of us have stopped listening to new music. Meanwhile, popular songs released when you're in your early teens are likely to remain quite popular among your age group through the rest of your life.
