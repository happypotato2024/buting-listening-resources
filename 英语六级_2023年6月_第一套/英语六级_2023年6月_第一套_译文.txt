[p26] 原文：Think about something as simple as buying a coffee. That may not seem like an experience that would make a deep impression on your memory, but anxiety could change that. That in turn may make them able to better remember some events.
[p26] 译文：想想一些像买咖啡这样简单的事情。这看起来可能不会给你的记忆留下深刻的印象，但焦虑可能会改变这一点。事实上，一项新的研究表明，焦虑水平较高的人可能比焦虑水平较低的人更好地记住某些信息。这是因为较高水平的焦虑可能会使人们更容易受到负面情绪的影响，从而使他们处于更消极的心态。这反过来可能会让他们能够更好地记住一些事件。

[p27] 原文：Let's take a closer look at that new study now. In this study, the researchers started by giving 80 undergraduate students an anxiety test. The test measured the participants' anxiety levels over the preceding two weeks. Then, to test memory, the participants were shown a series of neutral words, one at a time. Some of the words were printed on to photos of negative scenes, meaning images that could affect their emotions negatively, such as a photo of a car accident or a cemetery. The rest of the words were printed on to photos of neutral scenes, such as a photo of a lake or trees. Neutral words included words like "table" or "desk" that don't elicit emotion. Later, the participants were asked to think back to the words they were shown earlier, which caused them to reenter either a negative or a neutral mindset. The participants were then presented with another set of neutral words, and their memory of these new words was tested. The researchers found that the new words presented to people in a negative mindset were better remembered by people with higher levels of anxiety than those with lower levels of anxiety. In other words, when highly anxious individuals took in otherwise emotionally neutral information that was presented to them, it became colored by their negative mindset, making them remember the information better. But these same effects were not seen in people with low levels of anxiety. Previous studies have found that extreme levels of anxiety, such as those experienced by people diagnosed with an anxiety disorder, can be quite detrimental to memory and cognitive performance. But the highly anxious people in this study represent individuals who are managing their anxiety and for whom anxiety is not a serious problem.
[p27] 译文：现在让我们仔细看看这项新研究。在这项研究中，研究人员首先对 80 名本科生进行了焦虑测试。该测试测量了参与者在过去两周内的焦虑水平。然后，为了测试记忆力，参与者会看到一系列中性单词，一次一个。有些文字被印在负面场景的照片上，这意味着可能对他们的情绪产生负面影响的图像，例如车祸或墓地的照片。其余的文字被打印在中性场景的照片上，例如湖泊或树木的照片。中性词包括“桌子”或“书桌”等不会引起情绪的词。后来，参与者被要求回想之前看到的话语，这导致他们重新进入消极或中立的心态。然后向参与者提供另一组中性词，并测试他们对这些新词的记忆。研究人员发现，与焦虑水平较低的人相比，焦虑水平较高的人更容易记住向处于消极心态的人展示的新单词。换句话说，当高度焦虑的人接受了呈现给他们的情绪中性的信息时，这些信息就会被他们的消极心态所影响，从而使他们更好地记住这些信息。但在焦虑水平较低的人身上却没有看到同样的效果。先前的研究发现，极端程度的焦虑，例如被诊断患有焦虑症的人所经历的焦虑，可能会对记忆和认知能力造成相当大的损害。但这项研究中高度焦虑的人代表的是正在控制焦虑的人，并且对他们来说焦虑并不是一个严重的问题。
