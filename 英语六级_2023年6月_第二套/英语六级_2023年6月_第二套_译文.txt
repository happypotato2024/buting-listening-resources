讲座一

[p28] 原文：On average, US workers spend about 10% of their workday surfing the internet, emailing friends or shopping online. This behavior, called cyberloafing, is believed to cost employers up to $85 billion a year in lost productivity.
[p28] 译文：平均而言，美国工人每天大约花 10% 的时间上网、给朋友发电子邮件或网上购物。这种被称为“网络闲逛”的行为据信每年会给雇主带来高达 850 亿美元的生产力损失。

[p29] 原文：The majority of cyberloafing research focuses on ways to prevent employees from engaging in this behavior through interventions such as internet monitoring and computer use policies. But it turns out, such behaviors may not be a sign that a worker is lazy or just wasting time. Social media use at work has been linked to higher levels of employee engagement and job satisfaction. New research suggests cyberloafing can help workers cope with an exceptionally stressful work environment.
[p29] 译文：大多数网络闲置研究的重点是如何通过互联网监控和计算机使用政策等干预措施来防止员工参与这种行为。但事实证明，这种行为可能并不表明员工懒惰或只是浪费时间。工作中社交媒体的使用与更高水平的员工敬业度和工作满意度有关。新研究表明，网络闲逛可以帮助员工应对异常紧张的工作环境。

[p30] 原文：But is cyberloafing actually effective at reducing employee stress levels? That's the question my research team wanted to answer in our new study. Our hypothesis was that cyberloafing may serve as a mini-break, giving employees an opportunity to recover from stressful work situations. To test this, we asked workers to complete an online survey and rank how much time they spent cyberloafing, checking non-work emails and shopping. We also asked them to rank job satisfaction, their desire to quit, and how often they've experienced mistreatment at work, such as being bullied, threatened or yelled at. As you might expect, we found that overall, more mistreatment at work was correlated with lower levels of job satisfaction and a greater desire to quit. More interestingly, we found that cyberloafing effectively buffered this connection. That is, mistreated workers who spent more time surfing the web and checking emails reported higher job satisfaction and were less likely to want to quit than similar participants who didn't cyberloaf as much. This suggests that cyberloafing acts as a sort of relief valve for workers, helping them recover from stressful experiences.
[p30] 译文：但网络闲逛真的能有效减轻员工的压力吗？这就是我的研究团队想要在我们的新研究中回答的问题。我们的假设是，网络闲逛可以作为一种短暂的休息，让员工有机会从紧张的工作环境中恢复过来。为了测试这一点，我们要求员工完成一项在线调查，并对他们花在网上闲逛、检查非工作电子邮件和购物的时间进行排名。我们还要求他们对工作满意度、辞职意愿以及在工作中遭受虐待（例如被欺负、威胁或吼叫）的频率进行排名。正如您所料，我们发现总体而言，工作中更多的虐待与较低的工作满意度和更大的辞职意愿相关。更有趣的是，我们发现网络游荡有效地缓冲了这种连接。也就是说，与不经常上网的类似参与者相比，那些花更多时间上网和检查电子邮件的受虐待员工的工作满意度更高，并且不太愿意辞职。这表明网络闲散对员工来说是一种减压阀，可以帮助他们从压力经历中恢复过来。

[p31] 原文：While we did not directly assess how cyberloafing affects worker performance, we believe by relieving stress it may not only reduce worker turnover, but also ultimately bolster productivity. This fits with other recent research that suggests taking short breaks is associated with higher levels of daily job performance. That isn't to say cyberloafing is always good. Too much time spent on non-work activities likely causes performance to suffer. All in all, managers should be more lenient with employees. We believe a bit of online shopping or surfing the internet at work could make workers more productive in the long run.
[p31] 译文：虽然我们没有直接评估网络闲散如何影响员工绩效，但我们相信，通过缓解压力，它不仅可以减少员工流动率，而且最终可以提高生产力。这与最近的其他研究相一致，这些研究表明短暂休息与更高水平的日常工作绩效相关。这并不是说网络闲逛总是好的。在非工作活动上花费太多时间可能会导致绩效下降。总而言之，管理者应该对员工更加宽容。我们相信，从长远来看，在工作中上网购物或上网可以提高员工的工作效率。

[p32] 原文：When Julius Morton moved to Nebraska City in 1854, he faced a problem shared by many settlers in the territory: It was a treeless plain. [p32] 译文：1854 年，当朱利叶斯·莫顿 (Julius Morton) 搬到内布拉斯加州时，他面临着该地区许多定居者共同面临的问题：这是一片没有树木的平原。这意味着没有树木可以用作建筑材料或燃料。但莫顿是世界上最早的自然资源保护主义者之一，他说：“为了繁荣，我们应该种植与我们耗尽和消耗的森林一样多的森林”。于是他开始植树，从自己的土地开始。到 1860 年，莫顿拥有一片拥有 300 多棵树的森林。几年后，他的数量已经超过了1000个。

[p33] 原文：As the forest grew, so did Morton's influence in Nebraska. He also organized the Nebraska State Gardening Society and served on the Nebraska State Board of Agriculture.
[p33] 译文：随着森林的发展，莫顿在内布拉斯加州的影响力也随之扩大。这主要是因为莫顿创办了该州第一份报纸《内布拉斯加州城市新闻》，他经常在其中撰写有关植树的实用和美学益处的社论。他还组织了内布拉斯加州园艺协会，并在内布拉斯加州农业委员会任职。

[p34] 原文：While serving on the Board, Morton came up with an idea to spread his belief in tree planting statewide. On January 4, 1872, Morton drafted a resolution that April 10 be designated day for the planting of trees in the State of Nebraska and urged people to go out and plant trees themselves.
[p34] 译文：在董事会任职期间，莫顿想出了一个想法，在全州范围内传播他的植树信念。 1872年1月4日，莫顿起草了一项决议，将4月10日定为内布拉斯加州的植树日，并敦促人们外出植树。

[p35] 原文：Morton called the special event Arbor Day as "arbor" is Latin for "tree". The state government agreed. That day, an astounding one million trees were planted in Nebraska—an average of more than six for every man, woman and child in the state. [p35] 译文：莫顿将这一特殊活动称为“植树节”，“arbor”在拉丁语中是“树”的意思。州政府同意了。 1872 年 4 月 10 日，内布拉斯加州庆祝了第一个非官方的植树节。奖励植树数量最多的县、市和个人。那天，内布拉斯加州种植了令人震惊的一百万棵树——该州每个男人、女人和儿童平均种植了六棵树以上。自 1885 年以来，内布拉斯加州已种植了超过 700,000 英亩的树木，赢得了“植树之州”的绰号。

[p36] 原文：Arbor Day became a legal, civic holiday in the state in 1885. It was held on April 22—Julius Morton's birthday. In addition to a parade in Nebraska City, Morton introduced what has since become a long-standing Arbor Day tradition: Schoolchildren went outside and planted trees together.

[p37] 译文：1970 年，在首次庆祝植树节近一个世纪后，理查德·尼克松总统宣布每年四月的最后一个星期五为国家植树节。所有 50 个州都承认四月份的庆祝活动，尽管许多州在更适合当地植树的一个月内额外举办了植树节。

在非工作活动上花费太多时间可能会导致绩效下降。

[p31s4]
All in all, managers should be more lenient with employees.
总而言之，管理者应该对员工更加宽容。

[p31s5]
We believe a bit of online shopping or surfing the internet at work could make workers more productive in the long run.
我们相信，从长远来看，在工作中上网购物或上网可以提高员工的工作效率。

[p32s0]
When Julius Morton moved to Nebraska City in 1854, he faced a problem shared by many settlers in the territory: It was a treeless plain.
1854 年，当朱利叶斯·莫顿 (Julius Morton) 搬到内布拉斯加州时，他面临着该地区许多定居者共同面临的问题：这是一片没有树木的平原。

[p32s1]
That meant no trees for building materials, or for fuel.
这意味着没有树木可以用作建筑材料或燃料。

[p32s2]
But Morton was one of the world's first conservationists, stating, "For prosperity, we ought to plant as many forests as we have exhausted and consumed".
但莫顿是世界上最早的自然资源保护主义者之一，他说：“为了繁荣，我们应该种植与我们耗尽和消耗的森林一样多的森林”。

[p32s3]
So he started planting trees, beginning with his own land.
于是他开始植树，从自己的土地开始。

[p32s4]
By 1860 Morton possessed a forest of more than 300 trees.
到 1860 年，莫顿拥有一片拥有 300 多棵树的森林。

[p32s5]
A few years later, he had more than 1,000.
几年后，他的数量已经超过了1000个。

[p33s0]
As the forest grew, so did Morton's influence in Nebraska.
随着森林的发展，莫顿在内布拉斯加州的影响力也随之扩大。

[p33s1]
This was largely because Morton founded the Nebraska City News, the state's first newspaper, in which he frequently wrote editorials about the practical and aesthetic benefits of tree planting.
这主要是因为莫顿创办了该州第一份报纸《内布拉斯加州城市新闻》，他经常在其中撰写有关植树的实用和美学益处的社论。

[p33s2]
He also organized the Nebraska State Gardening Society and served on the Nebraska State Board of Agriculture.
他还组织了内布拉斯加州园艺协会，并在内布拉斯加州农业委员会任职。

[p34s0]
While serving on the Board, Morton came up with an idea to spread his belief in tree planting statewide.
在董事会任职期间，莫顿想出了一个想法，在全州范围内传播他的植树信念。

[p34s1]
On January 4, 1872, Morton drafted a resolution that April 10 be designated day for the planting of trees in the State of Nebraska and urged people to go out and plant trees themselves.
1872年1月4日，莫顿起草了一项决议，将4月10日定为内布拉斯加州的植树日，并敦促人们外出植树。

[p35s0]
Morton called the special event Arbor Day as "arbor" is Latin for "tree".
莫顿将这一特殊活动称为“植树节”，“arbor”在拉丁语中是“树”的意思。

[p35s1]
The state government agreed.
州政府同意了。

[p35s2]
And on April 10, 1872, the first unofficial Arbor Day was celebrated throughout Nebraska.
1872 年 4 月 10 日，内布拉斯加州庆祝了第一个非官方的植树节。

[p35s3]
Prizes were awarded to counties, cities, and individuals who planted the largest number of trees.
奖励植树数量最多的县、市和个人。

[p35s4]
That day, an astounding one million trees were planted in Nebraska—an average of more than six for every man, woman and child in the state.
那天，内布拉斯加州种植了令人震惊的一百万棵树——该州每个男人、女人和儿童平均种植了六棵树以上。

[p35s5]
Since 1885, Nebraska has planted more than 700,000 acres of trees, earning it the nickname "the Tree Planters State".
自 1885 年以来，内布拉斯加州已种植了超过 700,000 英亩的树木，赢得了“植树之州”的绰号。

[p36s0]
Arbor Day became a legal, civic holiday in the state in 1885.
1885 年，植树节成为该州的法定公民假日。

[p36s1]
It was held on April 22—Julius Morton's birthday.
4 月 22 日是朱利叶斯·莫顿 (Julius Morton) 的生日。

[p36s2]
In addition to a parade in Nebraska City, Morton introduced what has since become a long-standing Arbor Day tradition: Schoolchildren went outside and planted trees together.
除了在内布拉斯加州市举行游行之外，莫顿还介绍了植树节的一项长期传统：学生们到户外一起植树。

[p37s0]
In 1970, nearly a century after Arbor Day was first celebrated, President Richard Nixon declared the last Friday of every April to be observed as National Arbor Day.
1970 年，在首次庆祝植树节近一个世纪后，理查德·尼克松总统宣布每年四月的最后一个星期五为国家植树节。

[p37s1]
All 50 states recognize the April observance, although many hold an additional state Arbor Day in a month more suited to local tree planting.
所有 50 个州都承认四月份的庆祝活动，尽管许多州在更适合当地植树的一个月内额外举办了植树节。

[p38s0]
Modern humans arose in Africa about 200,000 years ago.
现代人类大约20万年前出现在非洲。

[p38s1]
They then spread across Europe and Asia sometime after 60,000 years ago.
六万年前的某个时候，它们传播到了欧洲和亚洲。

[p38s2]
This is the "Out of Africa" model, as it's commonly known.
这就是众所周知的“走出非洲”模式。

[p38s3]
In the 1990s, the hypothesis found widespread acceptance.
20 世纪 90 年代，这一假说被广泛接受。

[p38s4]
But this popular idea is in need of revision, particularly given the number of important findings across Asia over the past few decades.
但这种流行的想法需要修改，特别是考虑到过去几十年来亚洲各地出现了大量重要发现。

[p39s0]
For instance, many new human fossils found, particularly in China, are now dated as older than 60,000 years.
例如，许多新发现的人类化石，特别是在中国发现的化石，其历史已有 6 万多年的历史。

[p39s1]
This calls into question the idea that modern humans migrated out of Africa only 60,000 years ago.
这对现代人类仅在六万年前迁出非洲的观点提出了质疑。

[p39s2]
Take the recent discovery of two modern human teeth found in the Luna cave in China's Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region.
以最近在中国广西壮族自治区月神洞发现的两颗现代人类牙齿为例。

[p39s3]
When my research team and I dated these teeth, we found they were more than 70,000 years old—a situation clearly impossible if modern humans moved out of Africa only 60,000 years ago.
当我和我的研究团队对这些牙齿进行年代测定时，我们发现它们已有 7 万多年的历史——如果现代人类在 6 万年前才离开非洲，这种情况显然是不可能的。

[p40s0]
So with such findings, what happened exactly?
那么有了这样的发现，究竟发生了什么？

[p40s1]
Where does the most current data suggest we came from?
最新数据表明我们来自哪里？

[p41s0]
The first question we should ask is why did modern humans leave Africa to begin with?
我们要问的第一个问题是，现代人类为什么要离开非洲？

[p41s1]
If a population is perfectly adapted to a particular environment and has access to an abundance of resources, then there really is no reason to move or change.
如果一个群体完全适应了特定的环境并且能够获得丰富的资源，那么就没有理由迁移或改变。

[p41s2]
For instance, some monkeys in Africa have a set of teeth that has hardly changed over the course of millions of years, indicating that they found a place that has worked for them.
例如，非洲的一些猴子有一套牙齿，在数百万年的时间里几乎没有改变，这表明它们找到了一个适合它们的地方。

[p42s0]
So what happened with humans?
那么人类到底发生了什么？

[p42s1]
Some researchers have suggested that population density increased to the point where smaller human groups were forced to explore new lands.
一些研究人员认为，人口密度增加到了较小的人类群体被迫探索新土地的程度。

[p42s2]
Other researchers have suggested that due to major environmental events in East Africa, humans were prompted to find greener pastures.
其他研究人员认为，由于东非发生的重大环境事件，促使人类寻找更绿色的牧场。

[p42s3]
Yet another explanation could simply be that early modern human hunters were following the large animals that they relied on, and so they moved out of Africa without realizing that they were actually moving from one continent to another.
另一种解释可能是，早期现代人类猎人追随他们所依赖的大型动物，因此他们离开了非洲，却没有意识到他们实际上是从一个大陆迁移到另一个大陆。

[p43s0]
Humans need daily access to reliable fresh water, which appears to be absent from many coastal areas.
人类每天都需要获得可靠的淡水，而许多沿海地区似乎缺乏这种淡水。

[p43s1]
This brings us to another question: by which route did modern humans move out of Africa?
这给我们带来了另一个问题：现代人类是通过什么路线走出非洲的？

[p43s2]
No clear routes across the Mediterranean from North Africa appear to be present, so the earlier dispersals out of Africa and into Europe and Asia might have been across the Arabian Peninsula.
似乎不存在从北非跨越地中海的明确路线，因此早期从非洲扩散到欧洲和亚洲的可能是穿越阿拉伯半岛。
