讲座一

[p28] 原文：Different people use different strategies for managing conflicts. These strategies are learned in childhood. Usually we are not aware of how we act in conflict situations. We just do whatever seems to come naturally. But we do have a personal strategy, and because it is learned, we can always change it by learning new and more effective ways of managing conflicts. When you get involved in a conflict, there are two major concerns you have to take into account: achieving your personal goals and keeping a good relationship with the other person. How important your personal goals are and how important the relationship is to you affect how you act in a conflict. Given these two concerns, five styles of managing conflicts can be identified.
[p28] 译文：不同的人使用不同的策略来管理冲突。这些策略是在童年时习得的。通常我们不知道自己在冲突情况下如何行事。我们只是做任何看起来自然而然的事情。但我们确实有个人策略，而且因为它是后天习得的，我们总是可以通过学习新的、更有效的冲突管理方法来改变它。当您卷入冲突时，您必须考虑两个主要问题：实现您的个人目标以及与他人保持良好的关系。您的个人目标有多重要以及人际关系对您有多重要会影响您在冲突中的行为。考虑到这两个问题，可以确定五种管理冲突的方式。

[p29] 原文：1. The turtle. Turtles withdraw into their shells to avoid conflicts. They give up their personal goals and relationships. They believe it is easier to withdraw from a conflict than to face it.
[p29] 译文：1.乌龟。海龟会缩进壳里以避免冲突。他们放弃个人目标和人际关系。他们认为，退出冲突比面对冲突更容易。

[p30] 原文：2. The shark. Sharks try to overpower opponents by forcing them to accept their solution to the conflict. They seek to achieve their goals at all costs. Sharks assume that conflicts are settled by one person winning and one person losing. Winning gives sharks a sense of pride and achievement. Losing gives them a sense of weakness, inadequacy, and failure.
[p30] 译文：2.鲨鱼。鲨鱼试图通过迫使对手接受他们的冲突解决方案来压倒对手。他们不惜一切代价寻求实现自己的目标。鲨鱼认为冲突是通过一个人获胜和一个人失败来解决的。胜利给鲨鱼带来自豪感和成就感。失败会给他们一种软弱、无能和失败的感觉。

[p31] 原文：3. The teddy bear. Teddy bears want to be accepted and liked by other people. They think that conflict should be avoided in favor of harmony, and believe that conflicts cannot be discussed without damaging relationships. They give up their goals to preserve the relationship.
[p31] 译文：3.泰迪熊。泰迪熊希望被其他人接受和喜欢。他们认为应该避免冲突以利于和谐，并且认为讨论冲突就不能不破坏关系。他们为了维持关系而放弃自己的目标。

[p32] 原文：4. The fox. Foxes are moderately concerned with their own goals and about their relationships with other people. They give up part of their goals and persuade the other person in a conflict to give up part of his goals. They seek a solution to conflicts where both sides gain something.
[p32] 译文：4. 狐狸。狐狸适度关心自己的目标以及与他人的关系。他们放弃部分目标，并说服冲突中的对方放弃部分目标。他们寻求冲突的解决方案，让双方都能有所收获。

[p33] 原文：5. The owl. Owls view conflicts as problems to be solved. They see conflicts as improving relationships by reducing tension between two people. They try to begin a discussion that identifies the conflict as a problem. By seeking solutions that satisfy both themselves and the other person, owls maintain the relationship. Owls are not satisfied until a solution is found that achieves their own goals and the other person's goals, and they are not satisfied until the tensions and negative feelings have been fully resolved.
[p33] 译文：5.猫头鹰。猫头鹰将冲突视为需要解决的问题。他们认为冲突可以通过减少两个人之间的紧张关系来改善关系。他们试图开始讨论，将冲突视为一个问题。猫头鹰通过寻求让自己和对方都满意的解决方案来维持这种关系。猫头鹰不会满足，直到找到实现自己目标和他人目标的解决方案，直到紧张和负面情绪完全解决为止。

地球上所有 150 万种已知动植物物种的遗传密码将被绘制出来，以帮助拯救物种免于灭绝并促进人类健康。

[p34s1]
Scientists hope that cracking the genetic code of plants and animals could help uncover new treatments for infectious diseases, slow aging, improve crops and agriculture, and create new bio-materials.
科学家希望破解动植物的遗传密码有助于发现传染病的新疗法、延缓衰老、改善农作物和农业以及创造新的生物材料。

[p35s0]
In Britain, organisations including the Natural History Museum, the Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew and the Wellcome Sanger Institute have joined forces to sequence Britain's 66,000 species of animals and plants.
在英国，包括自然历史博物馆、邱园皇家植物园和威康桑格研究所在内的组织已联手对英国 66,000 种动植物进行测序。

[p35s1]
Dubbed the Darwin Tree of Life Project, it is expected to take 10 years and cost 100 million pounds.
该项目被称为达尔文生命之树项目，预计耗时 10 年，耗资 1 亿英镑。

[p35s2]
Once completed, all the information will be publicly available to researchers.
一旦完成，所有信息将向研究人员公开。

[p36s0]
Many scientists believe that Earth has now entered the sixth mass extinction, with humans creating a toxic mix of habitation loss, pollution and climate change, which has already led to the loss of at least 77 species of mammals and 140 types of birds since 1500.
许多科学家认为，地球现已进入第六次大规模灭绝，人类造成了栖息地丧失、污染和气候变化的有毒组合，自 1500 年以来已经导致至少 77 种哺乳动物和 140 种鸟类消失。

[p36s1]
It is the biggest loss of species since the dinosaurs were wiped out 66 million years ago.
这是自6600万年前恐龙灭绝以来最严重的物种灭绝。

[p36s2]
Scientists say that sequencing every species will revolutionize the understanding of biology and evolution, bolster efforts to conserve as well as protect and restore biodiversity.
科学家表示，对每个物种进行测序将彻底改变对生物学和进化论的理解，加强保护和恢复生物多样性的努力。

[p37s0]
Dr. Tim Littlewood, head of Life Sciences Department at the Natural History Museum said, "Whether you are interested in food or disease, the history of how every organism on the planet has adapted to its environment is recorded in its genetic makeup.
自然历史博物馆生命科学部主任蒂姆·利特伍德博士说：“无论你对食物还是疾病感兴趣，地球上每一种生物体如何适应其环境的历史都记录在其基因构成中。

[p37s1]
How you then harness that is dependent on your ability to understand it.
如何利用它取决于您理解它的能力。

[p37s2]
We will be using modern methods to get a really good window on the present and the past.
我们将使用现代方法来了解现在和过去。

[p37s3]
And of course, a window on the past gives you a prospective model on the future."
当然，回顾过去可以为你提供未来的前瞻性模型。”

[p38s0]
Sir Jim Smith, Director of Science at Wellcome said, "Try as I might, I can't think of a more exciting, more relevant, more timely, or more internationally inspirational project.
威康科学中心主任吉姆·史密斯爵士表示：“尽我所能，我想不出比这更令人兴奋、更相关、更及时或更具国际启发性的项目了。

[p38s1]
Since 1970, humanity has wiped out 60 percent of animal populations.
自 1970 年以来，人类已经消灭了 60% 的动物种群。

[p38s2]
About 23,000 of 80,000 species surveyed are approaching extinction.
在接受调查的 80,000 个物种中，约有 23,000 个物种濒临灭绝。

[p38s3]
We are in the midst of the sixth great extinction event of life on our planet, which not only threatens wildlife species, but also imperils the global food supply.
我们正处于地球上第六次生命大灭绝事件之中，这不仅威胁着野生动物物种，还危及全球粮食供应。

[p38s4]
As scientists, we all realize we desperately need to catalogue life on our fragile planet now.
作为科学家，我们都意识到我们现在迫切需要对脆弱星球上的生命进行分类。

[p38s5]
I think we're making history."
我认为我们正在创造历史。”

[p39s0]
John Donne, the English poet, wrote in the 17th century, "No man is an island, entire of itself; every man is a piece of the continent, a part of the main."
英国诗人约翰·多恩 (John Donne) 在 17 世纪写道：“没有人是一座孤岛，可以自全；每个人都是大陆的一部分，是主体的一部分。”

[p40s0]
Now, a British academic has claimed that human individuality is indeed just an illusion, because societies are far more interconnected at a mental, physical, and cultural level than people realize.
现在，一位英国学者声称，人类个性确实只是一种幻觉，因为社会在精神、身体和文化层面上的相互联系比人们意识到的要紧密得多。

[p40s1]
In his new book, The Self Delusion, Professor Tom Oliver, a researcher in the Ecology and Evolution group at the University of Reading, argues there is no such thing as "self", and not even our bodies are truly "us".
雷丁大学生态与进化小组研究员汤姆·奥利弗教授在他的新书《自我错觉》中认为，不存在“自我”这样的东西，甚至我们的身体也不是真正的“我们”。

[p40s2]
Just as Copernicus realized the Earth is not the center of the universe, Professor Oliver said society urgently needs a Copernican-like revolution to understand people are not detached beings but rather part of one connected identity.
正如哥白尼认识到地球不是宇宙的中心一样，奥利弗教授表示，社会迫切需要一场类似哥白尼式的革命，以了解人们不是孤立的存在，而是一个相互联系的身份的一部分。

[p41s0]
"A significant milestone in the cultural evolution of human minds was the acceptance that the Earth is not the center of the universe, the so-called Copernican Revolution," he writes.
“人类思想文化进化的一个重要里程碑是承认地球不是宇宙的中心，即所谓的哥白尼革命，”他写道。

[p41s1]
"However, we have one more big myth to dispose of: that we exist as independent selves at the center of a subjective universe.
“然而，我们还有一个更大的神话需要消除：我们作为独立的自我存在于主观宇宙的中心。

[p41s2]
You may feel as if you are an independent individual acting autonomously in the world; that you have unchanging inner self that persists throughout your lifetime, acting as a central anchor-point with the world changing around you.
你可能会觉得自己是一个独立的个体，在世界上自主行动；你拥有一生不变的内在自我，作为你周围世界变化的中心锚点。

[p41s3]
This is the illusion I seek to tackle.
这就是我试图解决的错觉。

[p41s4]
We are intimately connected to the world around us."
我们与周围的世界紧密相连。”

[p42s0]
Professor Oliver argues there are around 37 trillion cells in the body but most have a lifespan of just a few days or weeks, so the material "us" is constantly changing.
奥利弗教授认为，体内大约有 37 万亿个细胞，但大多数细胞的寿命只有几天或几周，因此“我们”的物质不断变化。

[p42s1]
In fact, there is no part of your body that has existed for more than ten years.
事实上，你身体的任何一个部分都已经存在了十年以上。

[p42s2]
Since our bodies are essentially made anew every few weeks, the material in them alone is clearly insufficient to explain the persistent thread of an identity.
由于我们的身体基本上每隔几周就会重新制造一次，因此仅身体中的材料显然不足以解释身份的持久线索。

[p43s0]
Professor Oliver claims that individualism is actually bad for society, and only by realizing we are part of a bigger entity can we solve pressing environmental and societal problems.
奥利弗教授声称，个人主义实际上对社会有害，只有认识到我们是一个更大实体的一部分，我们才能解决紧迫的环境和社会问题。

[p43s1]
Through selfish over-consumption we are destroying the natural world and using non-renewable resources at an accelerating rate.
通过自私的过度消费，我们正在破坏自然世界并加速使用不可再生资源。

[p43s2]
"We are at a critical crossroads as a species where we must rapidly reform our mindsets and behavior to act in less selfish ways," he said.
他说：“作为一个物种，我们正处于一个关键的十字路口，我们必须迅速改变我们的心态和行为，以不那么自私的方式行事。”

[p43s3]
"So let's open our eyes to the hidden connections all around us."
“所以让我们睁开眼睛看看我们周围隐藏的联系。”
